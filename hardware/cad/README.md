**Never edit an STL or 3MF by hand.** Change the Python source (or, for an R3 part, edit the STEP in
`source/r3_step/` in a CAD program, save it over the original, and say so in the commit), then rebuild.

## Fasteners: M2 only

Every fastener on the machine is **M2** (2 mm metric; not to be confused with module M2):

| joint | hardware | hole in the print |
|---|---|---|
| print to print, through | M2 machine screw + M2 nut + M2 washer | 2.4 mm clearance |
| into a print | M2 thread-forming (self-tapping) screw, 6-12 mm | 1.8 mm pilot |
| print to plywood | M2.2 wood screw, 13-16 mm (1.5 mm pilot drilled in the plywood) | 2.4 mm clearance |
| pinch clamps (W14, W15, W16, F12) | M2 screw: clearance on one side, 1.8 mm pilot on the other | - |
| rod set screws (W09, W10) | M2 thread-forming screw or M2 set screw | 1.8 mm pilot |
| 3.4 mm adjustment slots kept from R3 (W05, F06, X03, ...) | M2 screw + M2 washer (5 mm OD) | slot |
| M3 hex nut traps kept from R3 | M2 nut + M2 washer in the trap, or use the hole as a 2.4 clearance | trap |

Purchased parts whose own threads are not M2 are **held without using those threads**: the NEMA17s
by W15/W16 split collars (face holes M3), the load cell by F08 + F13 (M4/M5 holes), the camera is a
board camera with M2 holes. Remaining non-M2 threads are built into purchased parts (chuck and
coupler grub screws, pot bushing nut, fan screws if you use them) or are motion screws (T8 lead
screw, the M6 syringe drive rod). The optional/upcoming C01 tiles, D04 rail adapter and R05/R06
corner mounts keep M5 slots for 2020 extrusion; an M2 screw + washer works in C01, and the donor
XY gantry keeps its own hardware.

The R3 conversion is automatic (`source/m2_fasteners.py`): concave full-circle holes of 3.4 mm become
2.4 mm and 2.9 mm become 1.8 mm, plus per-part rules (W01 base holes 5.5 -> 2.4, Q02 fan holes
4.5 -> 2.4, S07/S08 3.2 -> 2.4). The log of converted parts is `m2_conversion_log.json`.

## Units and coordinates

* Millimetres everywhere. STEP files carry mm units; STL/3MF are in mm.
* Parts are modelled in their print orientation where practical.
* Core-cell layout coordinates: x along the mandrel (away from the chuck), y toward the camera
  (+) and feed (-), z up from the top of the plywood. Mandrel axis at z = 37.0 mm (W01 datum).
* GLB files keep the CAD coordinates (Z up, millimetres); the website viewers and the render harness
  rotate them -90 deg about X for display.

## Printer assumptions

Target: **Bambu Lab A1 Mini** (180 x 180 x 180 mm). Every machine part fits that envelope (QA
column `fits_a1_mini`). Any FDM printer of that size works. Slicer settings are recommendations,
not part of the geometry:

| setting | machine fixtures (W, F, X, C) | motor/bearing mounts (W01, W09, W10, W11) | fit tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| material | PETG | PETG | PLA acceptable |
| nozzle | 0.4 mm | 0.4 mm | 0.4 mm |
| layer height | 0.20 mm | 0.16-0.20 mm | 0.20 mm |
| walls | 3 | 4-5 | 2 |
| top/bottom | 4 / 4 | 5 / 5 | 3 / 3 |
| infill | 25-35 % gyroid | 40-50 % | 15 % |
| supports | only where noted in the part notes | as noted | - |

Soft jaws (X02, W07/W08 inserts, R03 fingers) are PETG carriers for silicone pads or cast silicone,
not printed elastomer. Mould parts S07/S08 are masters for silicone working inserts.

## Tolerances

Holes in the CAD are nominal plus a default clearance:

| feature | CAD size | for |
|---|---|---|
| M2 clearance / pilot | 2.4 / 1.8 mm | through screws / thread-forming screws into PETG |
| base screw clearance | 2.4 mm | M2.2 wood screws into the plywood |
| NEMA17 collar pocket | 42.6 mm square, 3 mm corner chamfer | 42.3 mm motor body (W15/W16) |
| load-cell channel | 13.0 mm | 12.7 mm bar (F08/F12) |
| 8 mm rod socket | 8.2 mm | h6 rod, light press |
| LM8UU bore | 15.2 mm | 15 mm bearing, slip fit |
| 608 pocket | 22.2 mm | 22 mm bearing, press |
| T8 nut body bore | 10.6 mm | 10 mm nut spigot |

Print **Z01** first; it contains test features for each of these. If your printer makes holes
undersize, use the slicer's hole/XY compensation rather than editing every part; if you must change
CAD, edit the constants at the top of `source/core_cell.py` (`BORE_ROD`, `BORE_LM8UU`, `M2_CLR`, `M2_PILOT`, ...)
and rebuild.

**Measure before final fabrication:** mini chuck length and diameter, T8 nut flange PCD, load-cell
cross-section, microswitch hole spacing, potentiometer bushing, camera-board hole pattern (W18 assumes
34 mm square), NEMA17 body size (W15/W16 assume 42.3 mm), F06 channel height.

## Materials

* **PETG** for anything near motors or under load (creep, heat).
* **PTFE** (tape or tube) on strip-contact surfaces: the F06 guide channel, Q01 tray liner, W05
  bushing.
* **Silicone** (1 mm sheet or cast, Shore 20-40A) for soft jaws and tip moulds.
* **Steel** for the mandrel (0.8 mm, bought), rods, screws. Never print the mandrel or a blade.
* Prepared veneer may carry residues from its (separate) preparation process; keep wetted fixtures in
  PETG/PTFE and rinse them after use.

## QA

`tools/build_cad.py` records for every part: file names, part ID, quantity, material, bounding box,
volume, watertight and winding consistency, connected bodies, A1 Mini fit, estimated solid PETG mass,
and separate columns `cad_valid`, `print_successful`, `physically_fits`, `mechanism_works`,
`process_validated`. Only `cad_valid` is filled automatically. CI (`validate-cad.yml`) re-runs the
mesh checks on every push.

Current result: 71 machine parts CAD-valid; S01 and T03 have two disconnected bodies (needs repair);
22 of 24 reference models watertight (PR22, PR23 are not; they are display references). See
[QA_REPORT.md](QA_REPORT.md).

## Core-cell layout and interference

`source/core_cell.py` places every Phase-1 part and purchased-part proxy (motors, rods, chuck,
bearings, load cell, camera) and intersects the solids pairwise. It also sweeps the carriage along
the axis (x = 20...170 mm in 5 mm steps) at guide angles -60...+60 deg. Results are written to
`source/core_cell_layout.json` and `software/seedforge/data/core_cell_limits.json`, which the recipe
checker uses. Proxies are simplified envelopes: zero interference in CAD is necessary, not sufficient.
