02 What SeedForge is

A manufacturing platform

SeedForge winds thin wet wood veneer onto a 0.8 mm steel mandrel with synchronized rotation and traverse, checks every coil with a camera, cuts and demolds it, and logs each carrier. It is the factory side of the idea, not the seed product.

Modular, buildable, cheap

Ten modules (M1-M10), each printable on a 180 mm printer, testable on its own and replaceable. Generic NEMA17 motors, A4988 drivers, an Arduino Uno and a laptop; one M2 fastener kit for the whole machine. The core cell is about $128 in parts if you already own the Uno, motors and drivers.

Honest about evidence

Every claim carries an evidence level. CAD that checks out is CAD-validated, not "working". As of this release nothing has been built; the build guide and test procedures are ready for the first Phase-1 machine.

SeedForge started as a UC Berkeley EECS 149 course project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the university or by the authors of the research paper below.

03 Why self-burying seed carriers matter

Seeds scattered on the surface, for example by aircraft, are exposed to birds, rodents, sun and wind; many never establish. Luo et al. (Nature 614, 463-470, 2023) showed a three-tailed wooden carrier whose coiled body responds to humidity: it unwinds when wet and rewinds when dry, and with its tails braced against the ground, the tip carrying the seed works its way into the soil over repeated cycles.

Making such carriers by hand is slow and inconsistent. Repeatable, logged manufacturing - same strip, same tension, same pitch, same turn count - is a precondition for testing whether they work at scale. That is the gap SeedForge addresses.

The animation explains the idea in our own drawing; it does not reproduce the paper's figures or data.

04 Complete manufacturing process

Prepared veneer (a separate, approved lab process) → M1 feed and tension → M2 synchronized winding → M3 clamp, cut and demold → M4 tail forming → M5 free drying → M6 seed, tip and three-tail assembly → M7 vision QA → M8 output, with optional M9 transfer and M10 drone cassette.

07 Winding animation

To scale for the paper-reference recipe: 0.8 mm mandrel, 2.5 x 0.5 mm strip, 5.0 mm per revolution, 7 active + 1 sacrificial turn. The counters are the exact microstep totals the firmware commands.

Geometry: why pitch and guide angle are recipe variables

First-order helix model (python -m seedforge.geometry). The course target - a 4 mm strip at 45 degrees - is predicted to overlap itself by about 1.1 mm, so it cannot be "no gap, no overlap". Wet wood compresses and slips: measure real coils.

08 Finished carrier 3D viewer

Reference geometry: 7-coil body of 2.5 x 0.5 mm ribbon over 35 mm, three tails (27, 29.5, 32.5 mm path length), tip and seed envelope. Dimensions are SOURCE-SUPPORTED; tail and tip shapes are RECONSTRUCTED. A plastic print of it is a fixture reference, not a working carrier.

12 Vision QA

      
An overhead camera images the coil as a silhouette against a back-light. A deterministic OpenCV pipeline (no machine learning, no cloud) measures pitch, turns, gaps, estimated overlap and axis sag, and sends one verdict to the controller.

    16 Optional aerial deployment concept
    
      
    Downstream of manufacturing, after Phases 0-7. A proven ArduPilot aircraft flies (budget option: an ArduPilot-supported F405 quad); the flight controller signals a $4 RP2040 payload controller at each waypoint, which owns the release gates and confirms each release with an IR sensor; a companion computer for terrain classification is optional. The payload kit is about $18. Every planting point has a unique ID in one pre-assigned sector, intent is logged before release, and uncertain points are never retried automatically.
